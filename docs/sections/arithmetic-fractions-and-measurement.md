# Arithmetic, fractions and measurement

**In one line:** you add, subtract, multiply and divide whole numbers, fractions and decimals, work with feet and inches, and read a tape measure, usually without a calculator.

This is the one skill area that shows up in nearly every pipe trades entrance test. UA Local 5's training center lists "fractions, rules measurement, converting feet to inches" for its math exam, and Local 211's posting describes a 25-question test on fractions and decimals. If you only have time to study one thing, study this.

## What it measures

Whether you can do the arithmetic the trade uses every day, quickly and accurately:

- adding and subtracting fractions with different denominators, such as eighths and sixteenths
- multiplying and dividing fractions and mixed numbers
- converting between fractions, decimals and percentages
- working in feet and inches, including borrowing 12 inches when you subtract
- reading a tape measure or rule marked in sixteenths
- simple ratios and scale drawings

Pipe is cut to fractions of an inch. A fitter who gets 5/8 plus 3/16 wrong cuts a piece that does not fit, so locals test this directly.

## How the questions look

Most items are short and multiple choice, with four or five options. You will see three styles:

| Style | Example of the kind of thing asked |
|---|---|
| Straight calculation | Add two mixed numbers, or divide a fraction by a fraction |
| Measurement | Convert inches to feet and inches, or add three lengths given in feet and inches |
| Short word problem | How many pieces of a given length can be cut from a stock length |

Tape reading is sometimes shown as a picture. In these notes we describe the tape in words so you can practise the counting without a picture.

## A method that works

1. **Write it down.** Even simple sums go on paper. Most errors on this section come from doing two steps in your head at once.
2. **Make denominators match before you add or subtract.** Convert everything to the largest denominator in the problem, usually 16ths or 32nds. For 3/8 plus 5/16, write 6/16 plus 5/16.
3. **To divide by a fraction, flip it and multiply.** 3/4 divided by 3/8 is 3/4 times 8/3.
4. **Change mixed numbers to improper fractions for multiplying and dividing,** and back again at the end. 2 1/2 is 5/2.
5. **For feet and inches, work in inches.** Convert both lengths to inches, do the sum, then convert back by dividing by 12. 7 ft 4 in is 88 inches.
6. **Simplify and check the options.** Reduce your answer to lowest terms, then find it in the list. If it is not there, check whether the options are written as mixed numbers or decimals.
7. **Estimate first.** Before you calculate, decide roughly how big the answer should be. If 7 1/2 ft is cut into 1 1/4 ft pieces, the answer is a bit over 5 and under 8. That catches slips.

## Traps that cost points

- **Adding denominators.** 1/4 plus 1/4 is 2/4, not 2/8. Only the numerators add.
- **Forgetting to borrow 12, not 10.** Subtracting 3 ft 7 in from 10 ft 2 in, you borrow a foot as 12 inches, so 2 inches becomes 14 inches.
- **Mixing up which number to flip.** When dividing, flip the second fraction, the one you are dividing by.
- **Leaving an answer unsimplified.** 8/16 will not appear as an option if 1/2 does.
- **Miscounting tape marks.** On a tape marked in sixteenths, the long mark halfway between two inch marks is 8/16, which is 1/2. Count from the nearest labelled mark, not from zero.
- **Using the wrong unit in the answer.** If the question asks for feet and inches, 87 inches is not the answer; 7 ft 3 in is.
- **Rounding too early.** Keep fractions exact until the final step.

## Timing

Local math tests vary, and some are untimed: UA Local 5 says its math exam "is not timed". Where there is a limit, these items are short, so aim for about 45 to 60 seconds each. If one takes longer than 90 seconds, mark it, guess, and come back. Nearly every item in this section can be done in under a minute once the method is automatic, so speed comes from practice, not from rushing.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from any local's test, publisher or prep site. Work them without a calculator. Ignore the width of the saw cut unless an item says otherwise.

**Item 1.** 3/8 + 5/16 = ?

A. 8/24 · B. 11/16 · C. 15/32 · D. 1/2

**Item 2.** A pipe 7 1/2 feet long is cut into pieces 1 1/4 feet long. How many full pieces does it make?

A. 5 · B. 6 · C. 7 · D. 8

**Item 3.** A run of pipe is 10 ft 2 in. A piece 3 ft 7 1/2 in long is cut from it. How much is left?

A. 6 ft 7 1/2 in · B. 7 ft 5 1/2 in · C. 6 ft 6 1/2 in · D. 7 ft 6 1/2 in

**Item 4.** 3/4 ÷ 3/16 = ?

A. 4 · B. 9/64 · C. 1/4 · D. 12

**Item 5.** A drawing uses the scale 1/4 inch = 1 foot. A pipe run measures 3 1/2 inches on the drawing. How long is the real run?

A. 7/8 ft · B. 12 ft · C. 14 ft · D. 16 ft

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: B, 11/16.** Change 3/8 to sixteenths: 3/8 = 6/16. Then 6/16 + 5/16 = 11/16. *Why not A:* 8/24 comes from adding the tops and the bottoms, which is never how fractions add.

**Item 2: B, 6.** Work in quarters of a foot: 7 1/2 ft is 30 quarters and 1 1/4 ft is 5 quarters. 30 ÷ 5 = 6. Check: 6 × 1 1/4 = 7 1/2 exactly. *Why not A:* 5 pieces would use only 6 1/4 ft.

**Item 3: C, 6 ft 6 1/2 in.** In inches: 10 ft 2 in = 122 in, and 3 ft 7 1/2 in = 43 1/2 in. 122 − 43 1/2 = 78 1/2 in. 78 ÷ 12 = 6 with 6 left over, so 6 ft 6 1/2 in. *Why not B:* 7 ft 5 1/2 in comes from subtracting the feet (10 − 3 = 7) and then taking 2 from 7 1/2 because 2 is the smaller number. You cannot take 7 1/2 inches from 2 inches, so you must borrow a foot: 1 ft 2 in is 14 in, and 14 − 7 1/2 = 6 1/2.

**Item 4: A, 4.** Flip the second fraction and multiply: 3/4 × 16/3 = 48/12 = 4. *Sense check:* how many 3/16 pieces fit in 3/4? 3/4 is 12/16, and 12/16 ÷ 3/16 = 4. *Why not B:* 9/64 comes from multiplying instead of dividing.

**Item 5: C, 14 ft.** Each 1/4 inch is 1 foot, so count the quarter inches in 3 1/2 inches: 3 1/2 × 4 = 14. *Why not A:* 7/8 comes from multiplying 3 1/2 by 1/4 instead of dividing by it.

</details>

- **5 of 5:** solid. Keep it sharp with a timed mixed set every few days and move on to your next weakest area.
