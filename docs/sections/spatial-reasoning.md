# Spatial reasoning

**In one line:** you picture objects in your head: folding a flat pattern into a box, turning a shape, counting blocks, or seeing what a fitting looks like from another side.

Spatial items appear mainly in aptitude batteries, such as the GAN test that Steamfitters Local 601 names and the Differential Aptitude Test that Local 189 in Columbus may use. Locals with a plain math test may not include them. Check [which test your local uses](../which-test-does-my-local-use.md) before you spend much time here. If yours is a battery, this section deserves real practice, because spatial skill improves quickly with drills.

The skill is not abstract for a fitter. Reading an isometric drawing, picturing a pipe run around a beam and laying out an offset all use it every day.

## What it measures

- **Folding:** which faces end up opposite each other when a flat net is folded into a cube, and where holes land when punched paper is unfolded
- **Rotation:** where a shape or arrow points after it is turned
- **Counting:** how many blocks are in a stack, including ones you cannot see, and which small cubes of a painted cube have paint
- **Views:** what an object looks like from above, from the front or from one end

## How the questions look

Usually a picture with four or five drawn options. As with mechanical items, every item in these notes is described in words so that it is exact. Turn each description into a small sketch. That turns a hard mental task into an easy drawing task, and you can do the same with scrap paper on most paper tests.

## A method that works

1. **Sketch the starting position.** Label faces or ends with letters.
2. **Move one step at a time.** For a cube rolled forward, write where each face goes. For a paper fold, draw each fold separately.
3. **Use fixed rules where they exist:**
   - On a cube net shaped like a cross, faces with exactly one square between them in a straight line end up opposite each other.
   - Each fold of paper doubles the number of layers, so one punch through all layers makes 2, then 4, then 8 holes.
   - On a painted cube made of small cubes, corner cubes have 3 painted faces, edge cubes have 2, face-center cubes have 1 and inside cubes have none.
4. **Eliminate options.** If two options are mirror images, decide on one feature, such as which side a mark is on, and cross off the wrong one.

## Traps that cost points

- **Mirror image versus rotation.** A turned shape is not the same as a flipped shape. Check whether the object has been rotated or reflected.
- **Rotating the wrong way.** Clockwise means the way clock hands move. Draw an arrow before you turn anything.
- **Forgetting hidden blocks.** In a stack, blocks underneath others still count.
- **Counting the same edge cube twice.** On a painted cube, work edge by edge: a 3 by 3 by 3 cube has 12 edges with one middle cube each.
- **Changing viewpoint by accident.** When asked for the view from the end, imagine standing there and looking along the object, not looking down on it.

## Timing

Spatial items are quick if you sketch and slow if you try to hold everything in your head. Aim for 45 to 60 seconds each. If a fold or rotation has more than three steps, sketch every step. It feels slower but is faster overall because you will not need to start again.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from any local's test, publisher or prep site. Sketch each one.

**Item 1.** A large cube is made of 27 small cubes, three along each edge. The whole outside of the large cube is painted. How many small cubes have paint on exactly two faces?

A. 6 · B. 8 · C. 12 · D. 24

**Item 2.** A flat net for a cube is made of six squares. Four squares form a vertical column: from top to bottom they are labelled T, C, B and X. One more square, L, is attached to the left side of C, and one, R, is attached to the right side of C. When the net is folded into a cube, which face is opposite C?

A. T · B. B · C. X · D. L

**Item 3.** An arrow points north-east. It is rotated 135 degrees counterclockwise. Which way does it point now?

A. North · B. West · C. South · D. South-west

**Item 4.** A square sheet of paper is folded in half from left to right, then folded in half again from top to bottom, so it is a quarter of its original size. One hole is punched through all the layers, near the middle of the folded square and away from every fold. How many holes are there when the sheet is unfolded?

A. 1 · B. 2 · C. 4 · D. 8

**Item 5.** Blocks are stacked in three layers. The bottom layer is a solid square, 3 blocks by 3 blocks. The middle layer is a solid square, 2 blocks by 2 blocks, sitting on the bottom layer. The top layer is a single block sitting on the middle layer. How many blocks are there in total?

A. 6 · B. 9 · C. 13 · D. 14

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C, 12.** Cubes with exactly two painted faces sit on the edges, not at the corners. A cube has 12 edges, and on a 3 by 3 by 3 cube each edge has one middle cube between two corners. *Why not B:* the 8 corner cubes have three painted faces.

**Item 2: C, X.** In the column T, C, B, X, faces with one square between them end up opposite each other. C and X have B between them, so they are opposite. T and B, which have C between them, are the other opposite pair. *Why not B:* B is next to C in the net, so it becomes a neighbouring face.

**Item 3: B, west.** Going counterclockwise from north-east: 45 degrees brings it to north, another 90 degrees brings it to west. 45 + 90 = 135. *Why not D:* south-west is 180 degrees away, which is a half turn.

**Item 4: C, 4.** Each fold doubles the layers: one fold gives 2 layers, two folds give 4. One punch through 4 layers makes 4 holes, one in each quarter of the sheet.

**Item 5: D, 14.** Bottom 3 × 3 = 9, middle 2 × 2 = 4, top 1. 9 + 4 + 1 = 14. *Why not C:* 13 leaves out the top block or miscounts a layer. Hidden blocks under others still count.

</details>

- **5 of 5:** strong spatial sense. Move on to picture-based practice so you get used to reading drawn options.
- **3 to 4:** find the item types you missed. Nets and paper folds improve fastest with a real sheet of paper: fold it and check.
- **2 or fewer:** sketch every item step by step. Make a paper cube from a cross-shaped net and label the faces. Ten minutes of hands-on practice teaches more than an hour of staring.
