# Mechanical reasoning

**In one line:** you work out how simple machines and physical systems behave: which way a gear turns, how much force a lever needs, what happens to water pressure and flow.

Several locals test this directly. Local 81 in Syracuse names the Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test, UA Local 343 in Vacaville gives a "Mechanical Aptitude" part, and the San Jose Pipe Trades Training Center says its exam "consists of basic math and mechanical reasoning". Aptitude batteries such as the GAN, DAT and Ramsay tests used by other locals also include it.

## What it measures

Common sense about how things move and push, backed by a few simple rules:

- **Gears and belts:** direction of turning and speed
- **Levers:** force and distance on either side of a pivot
- **Pulleys and ramps:** how they trade force for distance
- **Fluids:** pressure with depth, flow speed in wide and narrow pipes, levels in connected tanks
- **Stability:** what makes an object easy or hard to tip

You do not need physics training. The questions test reasoning, not formulas, although the formulas below make many items quick.

## How the questions look

Usually a picture of a simple setup with a question and two to four options. Bennett-style items often have three options, A, B and C, with C sometimes meaning "no difference". Because pictures cannot be shown reliably here, every item in these notes describes the setup fully in words. Draw a quick sketch as you read each one: that is also a good habit for the real test.

## A method that works

1. **Sketch it.** Two circles for gears, a line and a triangle for a lever. A ten-second sketch prevents most direction mistakes.
2. **Name the rule.** Before calculating, say which of the rules below applies.
3. **Use the rule, then sense-check.** Ask: should the answer be more or less than the input? A small gear driving a big one makes the big one turn slower. A longer lever arm needs less force.
4. **Watch for "no difference".** Sometimes two setups really are equal. Do not assume there must be a difference.

| Rule | In words |
|---|---|
| Meshing gears | Each gear turns the opposite way to the one it meshes with. Count along a chain: odd-numbered gears turn the same way as the first. |
| Gear speed | Teeth × speed is the same for two meshing gears. A gear with twice the teeth turns at half the speed. |
| Gears on one shaft | Two gears fixed to the same shaft turn at the same speed and in the same direction. |
| Belts | An open belt keeps both pulleys turning the same way. A crossed belt reverses the direction. |
| Lever | Force × distance from the pivot is equal on both sides when balanced. |
| Pulleys | With one movable pulley, two rope sections hold the load, so the pull is about half the load. |
| Ramp | Force × ramp length = load × height, ignoring friction. |
| Water pressure | Pressure increases with depth. The shape of the tank does not change it. |
| Flow | The same flow through a narrower pipe moves faster. |

## Traps that cost points

- **Counting the gears wrong.** In a chain of gears, count every gear, including idlers in the middle.
- **Swapping fast and slow.** The gear with more teeth turns more slowly, not faster.
- **Measuring the lever from the end, not the pivot.** Distances are always measured from the pivot.
- **Thinking a wide tank has more pressure at the bottom.** Only depth matters for pressure.
- **Ignoring "ignore friction".** Most items tell you to ignore friction and the weight of pulleys. Do so.
- **Forgetting the shaft rule.** Two gears on the same shaft share a speed even if they have different teeth.

## Timing

Mechanical items look harder than they are. Most take 30 to 60 seconds once you know the rules. If a test gives a picture-heavy section with many items, spend no more than a minute on any one, and guess and move on if you are stuck. There is rarely a long calculation.

## Practice items

These 12 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Bennett test, any other publisher's test or any prep site. Sketch each setup before answering. Ignore friction and the weight of levers, ropes and pulleys.

**Item 1.** Gear A has 20 teeth and turns at 60 revolutions per minute (rpm). It meshes with gear B, which has 40 teeth. How fast does gear B turn?

A. 30 rpm · B. 60 rpm · C. 120 rpm · D. 80 rpm

**Item 2.** Three gears sit in a row. Gear A meshes with gear B, and gear B meshes with gear C. Gear A turns clockwise. Which way does gear C turn?

A. Clockwise · B. Counterclockwise · C. It does not turn

**Item 3.** A straight bar rests on a pivot. A 300 newton load hangs 0.5 m from the pivot on one side. How much downward force at 1.5 m from the pivot on the other side balances it?

A. 450 N · B. 150 N · C. 100 N · D. 900 N

**Item 4.** A 200 pound load hangs from a single movable pulley. One end of the rope is tied to a beam above; the other end runs up from the pulley to your hand. About how hard must you pull to hold the load still?

A. 50 lb · B. 100 lb · C. 200 lb · D. 400 lb

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 4</summary>

**Item 1: A, 30 rpm.** Teeth × speed is the same for both: 20 × 60 = 1,200, and 1,200 ÷ 40 = 30. Gear B has twice the teeth, so it turns at half the speed. *Why not C:* 120 rpm swaps fast and slow.

**Item 2: A, clockwise.** A turns clockwise, so B turns counterclockwise, so C turns clockwise. The first and third gears in a chain turn the same way.

**Item 3: C, 100 N.** Balance means force × distance is equal on both sides: 300 × 0.5 = 150. The force on the other side must give 150 at 1.5 m: 150 ÷ 1.5 = 100 N. *Why not A:* 450 multiplies the load by the long distance instead of dividing.

**Item 4: B, 100 lb.** Two sections of rope hold the movable pulley, the one tied to the beam and the one you hold, so each carries half the load. *Why not C:* 200 lb would be right for a fixed pulley, which changes direction but gives no advantage.

</details>

**Item 5.** Water flows from a 2 inch pipe into a 1 inch pipe, with the same amount of water per minute passing through both. How does the water speed in the 1 inch pipe compare with the speed in the 2 inch pipe?

A. Faster · B. Slower · C. The same

**Item 6.** An open tank is filled with water. Gauge X is 2 feet below the water surface and gauge Y is 8 feet below it. Which gauge shows the higher pressure?

A. Gauge X · B. Gauge Y · C. Both read the same

**Item 7.** You tighten the same nut first with a 6 inch wrench and then with a 12 inch wrench, pushing at the end of the handle each time. To produce the same turning effect on the nut with the 12 inch wrench, you need:

A. twice the force · B. the same force · C. half the force · D. a quarter of the force

**Item 8.** Two pulleys are joined by a belt that crosses over itself between them, forming a figure eight. The driving pulley turns clockwise. Which way does the driven pulley turn?

A. Clockwise · B. Counterclockwise · C. It depends on the pulley sizes

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 5 to 8</summary>

**Item 5: A, faster.** The same flow has to pass through a smaller opening, so it must move faster. A 1 inch pipe has a quarter of the cross-section area of a 2 inch pipe, so the water moves about four times as fast. Think of putting your thumb over the end of a hose.

**Item 6: B, gauge Y.** Water pressure increases with depth, because there is more water above pressing down. Y is deeper.

**Item 7: C, half the force.** Turning effect is force × distance from the nut. Doubling the handle length halves the force needed for the same effect. This is why fitters use a cheater bar on a stuck fitting.

**Item 8: B, counterclockwise.** A crossed belt reverses the direction. An open belt, with no cross, would keep both pulleys turning the same way. Size changes speed, not direction, so C is wrong.

</details>

**Item 9.** A 400 pound load is pushed up a smooth ramp 10 feet long to a platform 2 feet high. About how much force along the ramp is needed?

A. 40 lb · B. 80 lb · C. 200 lb · D. 400 lb

**Item 10.** Two open tanks stand on the same floor and are joined near the bottom by a pipe with an open valve. Tank 1 is wide and tank 2 is narrow. Water is poured into tank 1 and left to settle. Compared with the water level in tank 1, the level in tank 2 is:

A. higher · B. lower · C. the same height

**Item 11.** Gear A has 12 teeth and turns at 90 rpm. It drives gear B, which has 36 teeth. Gear C, with 24 teeth, is fixed to the same shaft as gear B. Gear C drives gear D, which has 12 teeth. How fast does gear D turn?

A. 30 rpm · B. 45 rpm · C. 60 rpm · D. 180 rpm

**Item 12.** Four empty storage racks weigh the same. Which is hardest to tip over sideways?

A. 2 feet wide and 6 feet tall · B. 3 feet wide and 6 feet tall · C. 4 feet wide and 3 feet tall · D. 2 feet wide and 3 feet tall

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 9 to 12</summary>

**Item 9: B, 80 lb.** With no friction, force × ramp length = load × height: force × 10 = 400 × 2 = 800, so the force is 80 lb. The ramp is five times as long as it is high, so you push with a fifth of the load.

**Item 10: C, the same height.** Water in connected open containers settles at the same level, whatever their width. The narrow tank does not push its water higher.

**Item 11: C, 60 rpm.** A to B: 12 × 90 = 1,080, and 1,080 ÷ 36 = 30 rpm for B. C is on the same shaft as B, so C also turns at 30 rpm. C to D: 24 × 30 = 720, and 720 ÷ 12 = 60 rpm for D. *Why not A:* 30 rpm stops at gear B. *Why not D:* 180 rpm treats A as if it meshed straight with D.

**Item 12: C, 4 feet wide and 3 feet tall.** A wide base and a low height keep the center of gravity low and well inside the base, so it takes the most tilting before it tips. C has the widest base and is also among the shortest.

</details>
