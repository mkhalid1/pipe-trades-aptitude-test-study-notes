# Frequently asked questions

These are the questions people search for most about pipe trades apprenticeship tests. Several come straight from Google's "People also ask" box for US searches in November 2026; the rest come from the top searches and discussions on the same topic. Answers stick to what locals and the UA state, and say so when the answer is our guidance.

## What is on the pipefitter aptitude test?

It depends on your local, because each local picks its own test. Across the locals we checked, the content falls into five areas: arithmetic with fractions and measurements, algebra, reading, mechanical reasoning and spatial reasoning. Some locals test only math. UA Local 5, for example, describes a math exam on basic math, fractions, rule measurement and converting feet to inches. Others use a full battery. See [which test does my local use](which-test-does-my-local-use.md).

## Is the union pipefitter test hard?

The content is not advanced. It is mostly high school level math, reading and practical reasoning, and none of the published descriptions we found require trade knowledge. What makes it hard is that you often cannot use a calculator, fractions trip people up, and competition can be strong, so a bare pass may not rank you high enough. With a week or two of focused practice most people improve a lot.

## What math do you need to know to be a pipefitter?

For the entrance test: adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing whole numbers, fractions and decimals; percentages; feet and inches; reading a tape; and basic algebra such as solving 4x + 9 = 37. Some tests also include simple formulas such as the circumference of a circle or a right triangle. The [arithmetic](sections/arithmetic-fractions-and-measurement.md) and [algebra](sections/algebra-and-formulas.md) guides cover all of these. The UA says its apprenticeship coursework includes math, so you will keep building on this once you are in.

## How do I study for a pipefitters test?

1. Find out exactly which test your local uses.
2. Take the [15-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md) to find your weakest skill.
3. Work through the section guides for the skills your test covers, starting with the weakest.
4. Practise without a calculator, and write every step down.
5. Take at least one full timed practice test before the real one.

The [7-day and 2-day plans](study-plans.md) lay this out day by day.

## Is pipefitting hard to get into?

It can be competitive. Many locals recruit only once or twice a year and take a set number of apprentices. The New York State recruitment notices on our [which test page](which-test-does-my-local-use.md) list openings in single or low double figures for some locals. Selection usually runs through a test, an interview and a ranking list, so a strong test score improves your chances. Applying on time with every document the local asks for matters too.

## Is the plumbing apprenticeship test the same as the pipefitter test?

Within one local, plumbers, pipefitters, steamfitters and HVAC apprentices often go through the same entrance process. The New York notices for Local 13 and Local 22, for example, cover several of these trades under one committee. Between different locals, the tests differ. The skills are the same, so these notes work for either trade.

## Can I use a calculator?

Usually not, but it depends on the local. UA Local 5 says "calculators are not permitted" on its math exam. Many locals do not say either way, so assume no calculator unless your letter allows one, and practise that way.

## What score do I need to pass?

There is no single pass mark. Some locals publish one: Local 13 asks for 70% on its math exam, the San Jose training center asks for 75%, Local 22 asks for WorkKeys level 4 in each of three tests, and Local 7 asks for 50 on Wonderlic Basic Skills. Most locals do not publish one. Because scores often decide your rank, aim well above any minimum. See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## How many questions are on the test, and how long is it?

It varies. Local 211's posting describes a 25-question math test. UA Local 5 says its math exam takes about 30 minutes and is not timed. The San Jose exam allows three and a half hours. Figures such as "140 questions in 120 minutes" appear on prep sites, but we could not find a UA or local page that states them as a general rule.

## Do I need plumbing or pipefitting experience to pass?

None of the test descriptions we found ask for trade knowledge. The tests measure whether you can learn the trade: math, reading and mechanical sense. Experience can help elsewhere in the process, such as the interview.

## Can I retake the test?

That is set by each local. The San Jose training center allows three attempts in total and asks for a new application each time. Many locals test only during a yearly recruitment window, so a retake may mean waiting for the next one. Check your local's rules before you test.

## Where can I practise for free?

Every section guide in this repo has 5 original practice items with full explanations (4 in reading comprehension), and the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) has 10 more. For a timed mixed test, try the [free pipe trades practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/pipefitter-aptitude?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=pipe-trades-aptitude-test). If your local names a specific test such as WorkKeys or Bennett, also look for free sample items from that test's own publisher.
