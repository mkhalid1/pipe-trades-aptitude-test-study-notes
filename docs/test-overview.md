# The pipe trades apprenticeship test explained

**Short version:** there is no single "UA test". The United Association sets the apprenticeship standards, but each local's joint apprenticeship committee picks its own entrance test. What you sit depends on the local you apply to. The good news is that almost every test we found draws on the same five skills: arithmetic with fractions and measurements, basic algebra, reading, mechanical reasoning and spatial reasoning. Learn those five and you are ready for most of them.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Who sets the test | Your local's joint apprenticeship (and training) committee, the JAC or JATC. The UA says only that the process "may include an interview, aptitude test and physical requirements". |
| Trades covered | Plumbers, pipefitters, steamfitters, welders, HVACR and service techs, depending on the local |
| Common tests | A local math test, ACT WorkKeys, Wonderlic Basic Skills, Bennett Mechanical Comprehension, Accuplacer, the GAN aptitude battery, the Differential Aptitude Test (DAT) or a Ramsay test |
| Typical content | Fractions, decimals, measurement, algebra, reading, mechanical and spatial reasoning |
| Pass marks | Most locals do not publish one. Some do, for example 70% on Local 13's math exam and 75% at the San Jose Pipe Trades Training Center. See [scoring](scoring.md). |
| Calculator | Often not allowed. UA Local 5's training center says calculators are not permitted on its math exam. Check your local. |
| After the test | Usually an interview, then a ranking list. Scores often decide your place on that list. |
| Prior trade knowledge | None of the published test descriptions we found ask for plumbing or pipefitting knowledge. They test aptitude. |

Sources: the [UA's apprenticeship page](https://ua.org/career-paths/apprentice/) and each local's own notice, listed on the [which test does my local use](which-test-does-my-local-use.md) page and in [sources](sources.md). Checked 24 November 2026.

## Who decides which test you take

A UA apprenticeship is run locally. The local union and the signatory contractors form a joint committee, and that committee runs recruitment: it takes applications, sets the entrance test, interviews applicants and keeps the ranking list. That is why two locals a few hours apart can use completely different tests. One New York local uses Wonderlic Basic Skills, another uses its own math exam, a third uses WorkKeys and a fourth uses the Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test. All four are listed in New York State Department of Labor recruitment notices.

So the first job is to find out exactly what your local uses. The [which test does my local use](which-test-does-my-local-use.md) page shows how, with real examples.

## The tests you are most likely to meet

| Test | What it covers | Locals that name it (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| A local math exam | Arithmetic, fractions, decimals, measurement, sometimes algebra | Local 13 Rochester, Local 211 Houston, UA Local 5 |
| ACT WorkKeys | Separate tests named by the local, such as Applied Math, Workplace Documents and Graphic Literacy | Local 22 Western NY, Local 773, Local 43 Chattanooga, Local 190 Greater Michigan |
| Wonderlic Basic Skills | A basic skills test; Local 7 asks for 50 or higher to be interviewed | Local 7 Latham, NY |
| Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test | Mechanical reasoning from pictures | Local 81 Syracuse |
| Accuplacer plus the GAN aptitude test | Arithmetic, algebra, reading, plus a trades aptitude battery | Steamfitters Local 601 Wisconsin |
| Math plus mechanical aptitude | A two-part written exam | UA Local 343 Vacaville |
| DAT, Ramsay or another validated test | Aptitude batteries run by a testing company | Local 189 Columbus |
| Basic math and mechanical reasoning | One long sitting | Pipe Trades Training Center, San Jose (UA Local 393) |

## What the questions cover

Across all of these, five skill areas keep coming up. Each one has its own guide in this repo:

1. **[Arithmetic, fractions and measurement](sections/arithmetic-fractions-and-measurement.md).** Adding and dividing fractions, decimals, percentages, feet and inches, reading a tape. UA Local 5 lists "fractions, rules measurement, converting feet to inches" for its math exam, and Local 211 lists a 25-question test on fractions and decimals.
2. **[Algebra and formulas](sections/algebra-and-formulas.md).** Solving simple equations, putting numbers into a formula, rearranging it. Accuplacer and many local math tests include this.
3. **[Reading comprehension](sections/reading-comprehension.md).** Short technical passages and written procedures. WorkKeys Workplace Documents, Accuplacer Reading and the GAN battery test this.
4. **[Mechanical reasoning](sections/mechanical-reasoning.md).** Gears, levers, pulleys, pressure and flow. Bennett, the GAN battery, Ramsay and the San Jose and Vacaville exams test this.
5. **[Spatial reasoning](sections/spatial-reasoning.md).** Folding, rotating and picturing objects from other sides. Common in aptitude batteries such as the DAT and GAN.

## Where the test sits in the application

The exact steps vary, but most locals we checked follow the same pattern:

1. **Apply** during the local's recruitment window, with ID, a diploma or GED and transcripts. Some locals take applications only in person, on set days.
2. **Take the entrance test.** Some locals test on the day you apply. Others send you a test date later.
3. **Interview** with the committee if you reach the qualifying score.
4. **Get ranked.** Your test score, interview and other factors place you on a ranking list. Local 396 says applicants are "placed on the Ranking List based on their overall score from the application process", and the San Jose training center says "exam scores determine ranking on the waitlist".
5. **Get called** to start work with a contractor when your rank comes up.

Because the score often sets your rank, scoring well above the minimum can matter as much as passing.

## The one habit that matters most

Work carefully and show your steps on paper. Most of these tests do not allow calculators, the arithmetic is mostly fractions and measurements, and small slips cost more than hard questions. The section guides each give a method, a list of traps and practice items so you can build that habit.

## Practise the real format

Reading about a test is not the same as sitting one.
