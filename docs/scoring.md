# How scoring works

**Short version:** there is no national pass mark, because there is no national test. Each local decides how to score its own test and what score moves you on to an interview. Most do not publish the number. Where a local does, it is usually a percentage on its own exam or a level on a standard test such as WorkKeys. Your score often also sets your place on the ranking list, so the minimum is not the target.

## Pass marks that locals publish

These are the cut-offs we found stated on locals' own pages or in state recruitment notices, checked on 24 November 2026. They apply only to those locals and can change with each recruitment.

| Local | Test | Published qualifying score |
|---|---|---|
| Local 7, Latham, NY | Wonderlic Basic Skills | 50 or higher to be interviewed |
| Local 13, Rochester, NY | Math exam | 70% or higher |
| Local 22, Western NY | WorkKeys Applied Mathematics, Workplace Documents and Graphic Literacy | At least 4 out of 7 in each to be selected for interview |
| Local 773, NY | WorkKeys assessments | An average of at least 4.334 out of 7 to be interviewed |
| UA Local 343, Vacaville, CA | Math and mechanical aptitude exam | 70 percent |
| UA Local 396, Boardman, OH | Aptitude test | 70% or higher to reach the interview |
| Pipe Trades Training Center, San Jose (UA Local 393) | Basic math and mechanical reasoning | 75% or better |

Each is linked on [which test does my local use](which-test-does-my-local-use.md#real-examples-from-locals-own-pages) and in [sources](sources.md).

Notice how different they are. A 70% math exam, a WorkKeys level and a Wonderlic score are not on the same scale and cannot be compared. Ignore any website that gives one pass mark for "the UA test".

## What happens with your score

At most of the locals we checked, the test is a gate and then a ranking:

1. **The gate.** Reach the qualifying score and you move on to an interview. Miss it and the application usually stops there for that recruitment.
2. **The ranking.** After the interview, applicants are placed on a ranking or eligibility list. UA Local 396 says applicants are "placed on the Ranking List based on their overall score from the application process". The San Jose training center says "exam scores determine ranking on the waitlist".
3. **The call.** Apprentices are taken from the top of the list as contractors need them.

So two applicants who both "pass" can end up far apart on the list. A higher test score can mean an earlier start.

## How different tests report scores

| Test type | How the result usually looks |
|---|---|
| A local math or aptitude exam | A percentage correct, compared with the local's cut-off |
| WorkKeys | A level for each test. The New York notices above describe it as a score out of a possible seven. |
| Wonderlic Basic Skills | A score, which Local 7 compares with 50 |
| Bennett, DAT, Ramsay, GAN | Results reported to the committee by the testing company. Locals rarely publish the qualifying point. |

Whether you see your own score depends on the local. Some tell you the result by mail. Others tell you only whether you qualified.

## Is there a penalty for guessing?

None of the local pages we checked mentions a penalty for wrong answers. On most multiple-choice aptitude tests your score is the number correct, so a blank scores the same as a wrong answer. Unless your test instructions say otherwise, answer every item. If they do mention a penalty, follow them.

## What counts as a good score

If your local publishes a cut-off, treat it as the floor. Because scores often set your rank, aim well above it.

As our own study guidance, not a published standard: if you can score around 85% or more on timed practice that covers the same skills as your local's test, you have a comfortable margin for nerves and harder items on the day. If you are scoring near the cut-off in practice, spend another week on your weakest skill before you test.

## Retakes

Retake rules are set locally too. The San Jose training center limits applicants to three attempts at its entrance exam in total, and you need a new application for each. Many locals test only during their recruitment window, which may come once a year. Check your local's page before you test, and do not treat your first attempt as a practice run.
